## How Does Electronic Commutation in BLDC Motors Eliminate Mechanical Wear?

In traditional brushed motors, carbon brushes constantly rub against the rotating commutator, generating electrical arcing, acoustic whine, and physical friction that degrades motor brushes after roughly 150 to 300 hours of run time. In contrast, Brushless DC motors flip the architecture: powerful neodymium permanent magnets sit on the rotating rotor, while electromagnetic coils remain stationary on the stator. Hall-effect sensors detect rotor position, switching coil polarities electronically via microcontroller software with zero physical contact or wear.

## What Prevents Motor Stall Under Heavy Physical Squeeze Pressure?

When a user tightens their grip on an automatic stroker, mechanical resistance surges. Brushed motors suffer catastrophic speed drops under load because torque is limited by physical brush contact resistance. BOHOL's BLDC motor drivers incorporate closed-loop current feedback: when external resistance rises, the microcontroller automatically increases PWM duty cycle and stator current, maintaining a steady 350 strokes per minute without stalling or bogging down.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do some budget strokers produce an unpleasant burning smell after 15 minutes?
That burning odor is caused by physical carbon brushes overheating and vaporizing lubricant grease inside the motor housing. BLDC motors contain zero brushes and zero volatile grease, producing zero burning smell.

### Are brushless motor strokers heavier than standard models?
No. Because BLDC motors achieve much higher power density per cubic centimeter than brushed motors, the motor assembly is actually 20% more compact and lighter while producing 3x the usable output torque.
