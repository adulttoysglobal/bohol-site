A focused brief makes an early product discussion more useful. Rather than requesting a general quotation for an entire category, start with the exact models and the information you need to confirm.

## Identify your shortlist

Use the [catalogue](/catalogue/) to select the product names. Include the preferred color where it matters, and distinguish between a product you have seen in the gallery and a particular model number in a specification sheet.

## Describe your market and order

Include the destination market, sales channel and expected quantity. If you are exploring several quantities, list them separately. These details help frame the discussion without implying a fixed minimum order or a confirmed price.

## Separate the product from customization

If you are considering private-label or OEM requirements, describe the requested logo, packaging, colors or other changes as a separate part of the brief. Feasibility, timing, documentation and commercial terms need to be confirmed for the exact project.

Do not assume that an existing product photo demonstrates an available customization option.

## Ask for the right documents

Name the specific model, market and document you need. A report for a component or a submitted sample should not be treated as proof for every product variant.

## Keep a copy

The [business enquiry page](/partners/) lets you prepare and download a brief containing the selected models and your requirements. Keep that copy with any later correspondence so changes to the request remain clear.
