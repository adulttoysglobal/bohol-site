## 1. Signs of Physical & Mechanical Degradation

TPE is an oil-extended block copolymer. Over months of regular stretching and washing, the plasticizer oils gradually leach away, causing the material to lose its bounce. You may notice that the entry ring feels loose, or that the internal ribs feel mushy and indistinct rather than sharp and springy. In advanced fatigue, microscopic tears appear at high-stress points like the coronal constriction ring, causing the sleeve to rip during vigorous use.

## 2. Hygiene Expiration: When Washing Is No Longer Enough

If your sleeve develops an odor that persists even after thorough washing and cornstarch powdering, micro-fissures in the TPE have trapped organic matter beyond the reach of topical soap. Continuing to use an expired sleeve can lead to balanitis (inflammation of the glans), contact dermatitis, or urinary tract infections. Replacing the inner core restores factory-fresh hygiene and tight suction.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I recycle my old worn-out TPE sleeve?
Due to sanitary regulations, intimate elastomeric goods cannot be placed in household recycling bins. Wrap the sleeve in neutral paper and dispose of it in standard household waste.

### How can I extend the lifespan of my favorite sleeve?
Never pull aggressively on the entrance ring when washing, always use abundant water-based lubricant to reduce friction shear, and store the sleeve away from direct sunlight and heat radiators.
