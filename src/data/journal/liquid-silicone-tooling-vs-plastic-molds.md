## 1. Solid Compression Molding vs. Liquid Injection (LSR)

Lower-tier factories still use manual compression molding: an operator cuts a slab of solid high-consistency rubber (HCR), lays it manually into an open mold, and presses it under heat. This crude method results in thick parting lines, trapped air bubbles, and significant flash that must be hand-trimmed. BOHOL uses automated closed-loop LSR injection. Liquid Components A and B are metered under computer control and injected into vacuumed steel cavities, yielding flawless, flash-free surfaces straight from the press.

## 2. Dual-Shot Overmolding: Silicone Over Rigid Core

High-end devices like the [Starship](/products/starship/) or [Bobi III](/products/bobi-iii/) require an internal rigid ABS chassis to house the motor and battery, wrapped in a plush, soft silicone exterior. BOHOL’s dual-shot overmolding chemically bonds liquid silicone to high-temperature polycarbonate/ABS cores using specialized primer-less cross-linking agents, preventing delamination over years of vigorous vibration.

### How many units can a single LSR steel mold produce before wearing out?
Hardened S136 stainless steel molds running liquid silicone experience minimal abrasive wear and are warrantied for over 500,000 shots (production cycles).

### Can different colors and hardness levels be molded in the same tool?
Yes. The same mold cavity can shoot silicone ranging from ultra-soft 15A durometer to firm 50A durometer in any custom Pantone pastel or vibrant shade.
