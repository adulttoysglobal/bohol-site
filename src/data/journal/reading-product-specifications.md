A product specification is most useful when it is tied to an exact model and a clear source. Similar photographs or matching colors are not enough to establish that two products share the same technical details.

## Match the model first

Check the product name and any model number. The Mengqu specification, for example, lists MZB-WT054 for white and MZB-WT055 for pink. Keep those identifiers when asking about documentation or accessories.

## Check the units

Some product sheets use inches and others use millimetres. Make sure you compare the same units and understand whether a measurement describes the complete device, its packaging or another part.

Published overall dimensions do not establish an internal measurement. Where a source contains inconsistent labels, confirm the current specification rather than relying on an image alone.

## Read materials at model level

The Twist & Fun sheet lists ABS and TPE. Mengqu lists ABS and silicone. Those are model-specific material descriptions. They do not establish an independent certification or a universal material claim for the whole collection.

## Keep reports in scope

A test report describes the sample and tests identified in that report. A report for one sample or component should not be extended to unrelated products, batches or markets.
