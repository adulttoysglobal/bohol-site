## 1. How to Measure Girth & Calculate Nominal Width

Standard commercial products measure 180 mm to 200 mm (7.1 to 7.9 inches) in unrolled length, which fits nearly all users. Fit problems almost always stem from **girth (circumference)**:

1. Wrap a flexible tailor's tape (or paper strip) snugly around the widest point without compressing tissue.
2. Record the circumference in millimeters (or multiply inches by `25.4`).
3. Match your measurement directly against the complete ISO 4074 width matrix below.

## 3. Latex vs. 0.01mm Polyurethane Sizing Difference

Natural rubber latex stretches up to 700% elongation, making it forgiving across ±2 mm width differences. By contrast, ultra-thin **0.01mm waterborne polyurethane (PU)** conducts body heat far better and is 100% latex-free, but has lower radial elasticity—meaning buyers between two widths should size up by 1–2 mm when choosing 0.01mm PU.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are condom sizes in Canada the same as in the US and Europe?

Yes. Canada, the United States, and Europe all measure barrier sizing using flat nominal width in millimeters under ISO 4074 (typically 52 mm – 54 mm for standard retail packs and 56 mm – 60 mm for large packs), though European custom-fit brands print the exact millimeter number more prominently on the box.

### Why does the base ring feel too tight even when the length fits fine?

A tight base ring indicates the nominal width is 2 mm to 4 mm too narrow for your circumference. Moving up one nominal width tier (for example from 52 mm to 56 mm) or choosing a flared comfort profile relieves constriction immediately.

### What nominal width should brands stock first for retail or hotel vending?

For global retail and hotel vending fleets, **52 mm – 53 mm** covers roughly 65% of demand, while pairing it with a **56 mm Large Comfort** SKU captures over 90% of consumer sizing needs.
