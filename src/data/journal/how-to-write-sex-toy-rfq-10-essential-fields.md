## 1. The 10 Mandatory Fields Every Factory Engineer Needs

To receive a binding, transparent quotation without endless back-and-forth emails, structure your RFQ around ten core fields: (1) cooperation model (Stock Wholesale, Private Label ODM, or Custom Mold OEM), (2) target SKU list or 3D CAD/reference image, (3) material grade and Shore hardness, (4) waterproofing and motor/app functionality, (5) Pantone colorway and logo engraving specs, (6) retail box style and accessories (USB cable, satin pouch, manual languages), (7) exact order quantity tiers, (8) target destination markets and required certificates, (9) Incoterms (EXW, FOB Shenzhen, or Door-to-Door DDP) with destination ZIP, and (10) target launch timeline.

| RFQ Specification Field | Vague Buyer Email (Delayed Quote) | Structured 10-Field B2B RFQ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Product Scope** | 'Send me prices for vibrators' | SKU codes or 3D STEP file + target dimensions |
| **Material & Finish** | 'Good silicone' | Platinum-cured LSR, Shore 20A, matte UV velvet coat |
| **Order Volume Tiers** | 'Looking for wholesale' | 500 / 1,000 / 3,000 pcs tiered quotation matrix |
| **Compliance Target** | Not mentioned | USA (FDA + FCC + UN38.3) & EU (CE-RED + RoHS 2.0) |

## 2. How to Benchmark Quotes Across Multiple Suppliers

When comparing three factory quotes, normalize every line item on an apples-to-apples landed cost basis. Watch out for ultra-cheap unit quotes that silently downgrade platinum-cured silicone to peroxide-cured rubber, shrink lithium battery capacity from 600 mAh to 250 mAh, or exclude UN38.3 dangerous-goods air freight documentation.
