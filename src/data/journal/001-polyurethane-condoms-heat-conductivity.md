## How Does Polymer Crystalline Structure Enable 7x Faster Thermal Conduction?

Thermal transfer in polymers is governed by molecular chain density. Natural latex is an amorphous elastomer with loose, coiled molecular chains that scatter phonon vibrations, creating high thermal resistance. In contrast, medical-grade polyurethane features rigid aromatic urethane segments alternating with flexible polyol blocks. This tightly aligned, semi-crystalline lattice conducts thermal energy rapidly, allowing instantaneous sensation of a partner's internal body warmth upon contact.

## Why Are 0.01mm Polyurethane Condoms Stronger Despite Being One-Sixth the Thickness?

Consumers often fear that an ultra-thin 0.01mm condom will burst easily. In standardized tensile burst tests under ISO 23409, synthetic polyurethane exhibits tensile strength exceeding 65 MPa—more than double the tensile strength of natural latex (typically 25-30 MPa). Furthermore, polyurethane is compatible with both water-based and oil-based lubricants, unlike latex which dissolves when exposed to petroleum or massage oils.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are 0.01mm polyurethane condoms as effective at preventing STIs and pregnancy as latex?
Yes. Polyurethane is a non-porous molecular barrier with pore sizes far smaller than the diameter of viruses (HIV, HPV, Herpes) and spermatozoa, meeting or exceeding ISO 23409 and ASTM D3492 standards.

### Why do polyurethane condoms feel slightly less stretchy than latex?
Polyurethane has a higher tensile modulus (it resists stretching), which is why selecting the correct nominal width (e.g. 52mm, 54mm, or 56mm) is important. However, it quickly softens and molds to the body's natural contours when warmed by body heat.
