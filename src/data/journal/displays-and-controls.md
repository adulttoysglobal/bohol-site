The interface is part of the product’s design. Button placement and display shape can be as distinctive as the outer silhouette, but their functions should always be read against the exact model’s instructions.

## Three different layouts

[Twist & Fun](/products/twist-fun/) places its LCD panel above a vertical arrangement of exterior buttons. Its transparent window sits below the controls, separating the interface from the visible internal construction.

[CJ7](/products/cj7/) uses a green-accented control area running along the body. The supplied photographs show a digital display and a sequence of buttons within that outlined panel.

[Space](/products/space/) uses a circular display near the top of its cylindrical body, with exterior buttons underneath.

## A display is not a feature list

The presence of a screen does not confirm app support, remote control or a particular operating mode. Those functions must be checked in the model-specific documentation.

Likewise, a number visible in a promotional photograph should not be treated as a specification unless the written product information explains it. Product photography illustrates the layout; the manual explains how to operate it.

## Compare the whole device

When reviewing a product page, look at the front, side and rear views. Note where the controls sit relative to the body and how the form is constructed. Then read the specifications and the included instructions.
