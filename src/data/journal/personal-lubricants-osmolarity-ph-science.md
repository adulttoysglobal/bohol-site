## What Is Osmotic Shock and How Does Hyperosmolar Lubricant Damage Epithelial Cells?

Under basic cellular physics, water flows from regions of low solute concentration to high solute concentration across semi-permeable cell membranes. When a hyperosmolar lubricant (e.g. 3,000 mOsm/kg) is introduced into the vaginal canal (280 mOsm/kg), it creates an extreme osmotic gradient. Epithelial cells rapidly shrivel and dehydrate as their internal water is sucked out into the lubricant. This cellular collapse causes epithelial micro-fissures and immediate post-application burning.

## Why Is a Precise 3.8 to 4.5 pH Buffer Critical for Preventing Infections?

The healthy vaginal microbiome is dominated by *Lactobacillus* species, which produce lactic acid to maintain a protective acidic mantle (pH 3.8 to 4.5) that prevents colonization by pathogenic bacteria like *Gardnerella vaginalis* or *Candida albicans*. When neutral or alkaline lubricants (pH 6.0 to 7.5) are used, they neutralize this protective acid shield, triggering Bacterial Vaginosis (BV) and yeast infections.
